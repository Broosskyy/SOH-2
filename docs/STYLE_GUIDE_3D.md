# Stylized 3D Style Guide

## Style lock

Abyssal Dominion targets roughly 70% premium stylized fantasy 3D and 30% physical realism. Forms are believable and materials respond to light, but silhouette, value grouping and tactical readability win over tiny detail.

Do not drift into photorealism, mobile comic art, low-poly toys, tabletop dioramas, Clash-like strategy visuals or neon-saturated fantasy. Reference products inform readability and map composition only; their designs must not be copied.

## Shape language

- Player ships: confident bow, readable sail plan, warm wood/bronze, one faction accent.
- Enemies: darker hull/sails, sharper stern/bow rhythm and warmer hostile VFX.
- Monsters: one dominant silhouette and restrained emissive anatomy.
- Islands: broad natural masses, irregular shoreline, clustered landmarks and negative space.
- Props: chunky enough to read at combat zoom; small surface detail belongs in normals or color blocking.

## Color and light

Use deep blue/teal for open water, clearer turquoise near coasts and warm amber points around ports. Keep values separated: gameplay silhouettes must not merge with foam or UI markers. Light is art-directed and soft, with restrained rim light and no chrome-like wetness everywhere.

## Material character

Wood has broad grain/value breakup and high roughness. Sails are matte cloth. Metal and bronze carry focused highlights. Wet rock is darker and smoother than dry rock. Sand, reef and ruin stone remain desaturated enough that target colors and VFX dominate.

## VFX hierarchy

Muzzle flash is brief, smoke is readable but not opaque, projectile trails indicate direction, misses end with a water splash, hits produce compact splinters/sparks. Targeting and damage feedback must stay readable without becoming bullet hell.

## Zoom checks

- Close: material response, deck modules and water contact visible.
- Combat: ship class, faction and facing unmistakable.
- Tactical: silhouette, name/HP and target state survive; decorative detail may disappear.
